Game music in the 1990s came from the sound card's FM chip or from a MIDI
synthesizer, and the same game could sound completely different on each.
2ksbox has both.

## The sound card

Pick it in the machine's settings, on the **Audio** page.

| Sound card | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Sound Blaster 16 (with FM) | Windows 98 and DOS. Almost every game knows it. The default on both |
| Intel AC'97 | Windows XP. The default there |
| Intel HD Audio | Windows 7 and 11 |
| Gravis Ultrasound | DOS games that support it |
| AdLib (FM only) | Very old DOS games |
| Ensoniq AudioPCI (ES1370) | Other operating systems: BeOS, Linux, OS/2 |

The Sound Blaster's FM chip is a real OPL3 synthesizer, not a sample of
one. A DOS game set to "Sound Blaster" or "AdLib" music sounds the way it
did on the card.

## MIDI music

**Music (MIDI)** adds a MIDI port (an MPU-401) with a synthesizer behind
it, on Windows 98, XP, DOS and Other machines.

### General MIDI

**General MIDI (SoundFont)** is what a game's setup calls "General MIDI"
or "MPU-401". 2ksbox includes a sound bank, so it works out of the box.
Under **SoundFont (optional)**, you can pick a `.sf2` bank of your own,
which changes how every instrument sounds.

On Windows 98, Windows finds the port after you add "MPU-401 Compatible"
in *Add New Hardware* and pick it under *Multimedia* in the Control
Panel. It sounds much better than the FM chip Windows plays MIDI on
otherwise.

### Roland MT-32

**Roland MT-32 / CM-32L** is what a 1990 game means by "Roland". Many
Sierra and LucasArts games were composed on it, and they sound nothing
like General MIDI.

The MT-32 needs your own ROM images. 2ksbox doesn't include anything of
Roland's. Under **MT-32 ROMs**, pick the folder with your CM-32L control
and PCM ROM files. The machine won't start without them.

<Aside>
A game set to General MIDI or Roland with **No MIDI port** finds nothing
and falls back to FM or digital music.
</Aside>
