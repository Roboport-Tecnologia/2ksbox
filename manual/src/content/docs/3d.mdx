Games of the era drew 3D in three ways, and 2ksbox handles each one
differently:

| The game uses | What runs it | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Direct3D (DirectX 1 to 9) | The 2ksbox adapter, drawing on your graphics card | Install the guest tools once |
| OpenGL | Your graphics card's own OpenGL | Copy one file next to the game |
| Glide (3dfx) | An emulated 3dfx Voodoo 2 | Turn the card on and install 3dfx's driver |

Many games of the time offer two or three of these. Any of them works.
Direct3D and OpenGL are usually the fastest.

## Direct3D

Direct3D games need nothing per game. Once the guest tools are installed
(see [Your first machine](../first-machine/#install-the-guest-tools)),
the 2ksbox adapter's driver gives Windows 98, XP and 7 a DirectX 9 card
with hardware transform and lighting and shader model 3.0. Every
DirectX version from 1 to 9 runs on it.

On Windows 98, install DirectX 9.0c first. The DirectX 6.1 that comes
with Windows 98 SE has no Direct3D for the driver.

### How your computer draws it

The machine's **Direct3D** setting, on the **Display** page, picks how
your computer draws the game's frames. The note under it says what your
computer can do.

<Tabs syncKey="os">
<TabItem label="macOS">

On macOS 26 or newer, Direct3D runs at full speed through Vulkan
(**DXVK**). On older macOS it runs through Wine, if Wine is installed,
and the note says which Wine to get. Without Wine, games have no
Direct3D. They still have DirectDraw, OpenGL and the Voodoo 2.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Windows">

With a graphics card that has Vulkan 1.3, Direct3D runs through
**DXVK**, the tested path. On an older card, pick **This PC's own
Direct3D 9**. It runs on any card, and older cards often draw well with
it.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Linux">

With a graphics card that has Vulkan 1.3, Direct3D runs through
**DXVK**, the tested path. Without it, Direct3D runs through Wine if Wine
is installed, and the note says which Wine package to get. Without Wine,
games have no Direct3D. They still have DirectDraw, OpenGL and the
Voodoo 2.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

**Automatic** picks the best one your computer has. If the note says
"in software (slow)", your computer found only a software Vulkan. Try the
game both ways and keep the faster one.

<Aside>
Keep the 2ksbox adapter even when your computer has no Direct3D for the
machine. Only its Direct3D needs your graphics card. Its 2D, DirectDraw
and every resolution still work, and still beat the Cirrus card.
</Aside>

## OpenGL

OpenGL games, like Quake II and Half-Life, draw on your graphics card
through a small `OPENGL32.DLL` that goes next to the game's program
file. From the guest tools disc:

```
D:\SETUP /GAME 3 C:\GAMES\QUAKE2
```

Use the folder that holds the game's `.EXE`. You can also copy the disc's
`OPENGL\` folder there by hand. The guest tools install the other half,
*the device mapper*, with `/ALL`.

## Glide and the Voodoo 2

Glide was 3dfx's own interface, and many 1997 to 2000 games look their
best on it. 2ksbox emulates a real 8 MB 3dfx Voodoo 2 card, and the game
talks to it the way it talked to the real one.

1. In the machine's settings, on the **Display** page, turn on **3dfx
   Voodoo 2**.
2. Start the machine. Windows finds a new card.
3. Install 3dfx's own Voodoo 2 driver for Windows 98. The guest tools
   don't include it, so download it yourself.
4. In the game, pick Glide or 3dfx.

The Voodoo 2 draws at 640×480 to 800×600, like the real card. It runs
on your computer's processor, not the graphics card. Quake II runs at
well over 100 frames a second on an M1 Mac or a Ryzen.

**Voodoo3 undither filter** removes the speckled 16-bit dither pattern
the Voodoo 2 draws with, so skies and shading come out smooth. Unlike a
real Voodoo3's filter, it doesn't blur textures or edges.

DOS Glide games work too, in a Windows 98 DOS box or in pure DOS. They
bring their own Glide file (`GLIDE2X.OVL`).

## Windows 7 and Aero

On Windows 7, the 2ksbox adapter's driver gives the desktop Aero glass,
and Direct3D 9 games run on it as on XP. The guest tools' SETUP installs
the driver and turns Aero on.

## When a 3D game misbehaves

- **It seems frozen.** It's often a message box hidden behind the game's
  screen. Press Enter, or Alt+Tab to look.
- **Wrong colours or a garbled menu.** Try another way of drawing it: a
  different renderer in the game's own setup, or a different **Direct3D**
  setting.
- **It runs far too fast.** Old games often have no frame limiter. A DOS
  game may want a slower processor, which a DOS machine can have.
- **A keyboard that does nothing in a DirectInput game on XP.** FIFA 2000
  is one. Copy the DirectInput fix next to the game:
  `D:\SETUP /GAME 2 <game folder>`.
